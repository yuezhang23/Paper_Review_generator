This methodology uses one Large Language Model (LLM) as an "optimizer" to iteratively generate and refine prompts, and another LLM as a "scorer" to evaluate how well those prompts perform on a given task. The entire process is guided by a master prompt, called the "meta-prompt."

***

### **Step 1: Initial Setup and Scorer/Optimizer Designation**

The process begins by defining the core components for the optimization task. First, two distinct roles for the LLMs are established: an **optimizer LLM** (e.g., GPT-4, PaLM 2-L-IT) is chosen to generate new candidate prompts, and a **scorer LLM** (e.g., text-bison, PaLM 2-L) is chosen to evaluate the effectiveness of those prompts. A task-specific training dataset (e.g., from GSM8K or Big-Bench Hard) is also required to provide the accuracy scores that will guide the optimization. Finally, the process is initialized with a starting prompt, which can be as simple as an empty string or a generic phrase like "Let's solve the problem."

*   **Input:** A chosen optimizer LLM, a scorer LLM, a training dataset, and one or more initial prompts.
*   **Output:** An initial set of prompts with their corresponding baseline accuracy scores, calculated by the scorer LLM on the training set.

### **Step 2: Iterative Optimization Loop**

This is the core of the OPRO framework, where the optimizer LLM iteratively refines the prompts over multiple steps. The loop continues until a stopping condition is met, such as reaching a maximum number of steps or failing to generate better-performing prompts.

*   **Substep 2.1: Construct the Meta-Prompt**
    At the beginning of each iteration, a comprehensive "meta-prompt" is assembled and fed to the optimizer LLM. This meta-prompt is the sole input guiding the optimizer and contains three key pieces of information:
    1.  **The Optimization Trajectory:** A list of the best-performing prompts from previous steps, paired with their accuracy scores. The research found it most effective to include the top 20 prompts, sorted in ascending order of their scores (from worst to best).
    2.  **Task Exemplars:** A few examples (typically 3) randomly sampled from the training set. These show the optimizer LLM the specific task format and what a correct input-output pair looks like.
    3.  **Meta-Instructions:** A clear, natural language description of the goal, instructing the optimizer LLM to generate a *new* prompt that will achieve a higher accuracy score than those in the trajectory.

*   **Substep 2.2: Generate New Candidate Prompts**
    The optimizer LLM processes the meta-prompt and generates a new batch of candidate prompts. To improve stability and explore the solution space effectively, it is prompted to generate multiple distinct prompts (e.g., 8) in each step. A high sampling temperature (e.g., 1.0) is used to encourage the generation of creative and diverse solutions that are not just minor variations of previous ones.

*   **Substep 2.3: Evaluate New Prompts**
    Each of the newly generated prompts is evaluated by the scorer LLM. The scorer runs the prompt against the training dataset and calculates an accuracy score for each one. This evaluation is done with a temperature of 0.0 to ensure deterministic and consistent scoring.

*   **Substep 2.4: Update the Optimization Trajectory**
    The new prompts and their just-calculated scores are added to the historical list of solutions. This updated list, or "optimization trajectory," now contains richer information about what makes a prompt successful. This updated trajectory is then used to construct the meta-prompt for the next iteration in Substep 2.1, closing the loop.

### **Step 3: Final Selection and Testing**

Once the iterative loop terminates, the single prompt that achieved the highest accuracy score on the training set throughout the entire process is identified as the optimal instruction. To assess its true performance and ability to generalize, this final, optimized prompt is evaluated one last time on a separate, unseen test set. This final test accuracy is the primary metric used to report the success of the optimization process.

*   **Input:** The complete optimization trajectory containing all generated prompts and their training scores.
*   **Output:** The single best-performing prompt, along with its final accuracy on the test dataset.
